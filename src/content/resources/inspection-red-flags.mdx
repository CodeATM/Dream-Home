Inspectors find something in every house — that's the job. Most findings are negotiation chips: a $300 GFCI outlet here, a tired water heater there. But nine categories deserve your full, sober attention, because they're expensive in ways sellers rarely credit at the closing table.

## The quiet nine

1. **Foundation movement** — stair-step cracks in brick, doors that stick in frames, floors that slope enough to roll a marble. Repair ranges from $5K epoxy injections to six-figure underpinning.

2. **Water in the wrong places** — staining on roof decking, efflorescence in basements, soft subfloor near toilets. Water damage compounds; the repair bill grows every season it waits.

3. **Federal Pacific / Zinsco panels** — certain breaker panels have documented failure-to-trip histories. Insurance companies know this list; replacement runs $2–4K.

4. **Aluminum branch wiring (1965–73)** — fire risk at connections, requires remediation electricians and special insurance conversations.

5. **Polybutylene piping (1978–95)** — fails without warning, many insurers won't write it. Whole-house repipe: $8–15K.

6. **Buried oil tanks** — an undocumented tank is a soil-test lottery ticket. Remediation can run five figures.

7. **Unpermitted work** — that gorgeous basement suite may be a tear-out order if it's not legal. Check permits before you fall in love with finished space.

8. **HVAC past its service life** — a 25-year-old furnace isn't a defect until January, then it's $12K and an emergency.

9. **Sewer line failure** — clay laterals under big trees root-intrude constantly. A $150 sewer scope is the best money in real estate; repairs run $5–25K.

## How to respond

None of these automatically mean walk away — they mean *price the truth*. Get specialist quotes within your option period, then renegotiate credits or walk if the seller won't meet reality. Your agent has seen all nine before; let them run the numbers.

The houses worth buying are the ones whose problems are visible, priced, and boring.
