Rates are the loudest number in real estate and the least understood. Here's the whole picture in five minutes.

## Fixed vs. ARM

A 30-year fixed trades a slightly higher rate for certainty — same payment until you sell or refinance. A 5/6 ARM starts lower (often 0.5–0.9% less) then adjusts every six months after year five. ARMs make sense if you're confident about selling within the fixed window. Otherwise, pay for predictability.

## Points, translated

One point costs 1% of the loan and typically buys ~0.25% off your rate. The math: on a $600K loan, one point is $6,000 and saves roughly $95/month. Break-even: about five years. Staying longer than that? Points win. Might move or refinance? Skip them.

## Buydowns are back

Sellers can fund temporary buydowns — a 2-1 buydown cuts your rate by two points in year one and one point in year two before settling at contract rate. In slow markets, ask for this instead of a price cut; it can be worth more per dollar.

## Should you wait for rates to fall?

The honest answer nobody likes: waiting has its own cost. Prices keep drifting up in supply-constrained cities while you save for a down payment that's chasing an appreciating target. The classic line holds — *date the rate, marry the house*. You can refinance a rate; you can't refinance a purchase price.

## What actually moves rates

Mortgage rates track the 10-year Treasury and inflation expectations, not the Fed's overnight rate directly. When inflation prints come in hot, mortgage rates rise with them — often before the Fed does anything.
