Buying your first home feels like being handed the rules to a game mid-match. Here's the full board for 2026, in the order things actually happen.

## 1. Get real about budget (week one)

Pre-approval is not a suggestion — it's your ticket into serious showings. Lenders look at debt-to-income under roughly 43%, steady income history, and credit scores where every 20 points moves your rate. Aim to total monthly housing costs under a third of gross income.

## 2. Assemble your team (weeks one–two)

A buyer's agent costs you nothing in most transactions — sellers pay both sides. Interview two, pick the one who talks you *out* of houses sometimes.

## 3. Tour with a checklist, buy with your gut

You'll know the right house within ninety seconds of walking in. But bring discipline: foundation cracks, water stains on ceilings, windows painted shut. Cosmetics are negotiable; structure is destiny.

## 4. Offer strategy

Your agent runs comparables and advises on terms: price, earnest money, inspection windows, closing date flexibility. In multiple-offer situations, a clean offer with a solid pre-approval letter often beats a higher messy one.

## 5. Inspection, appraisal, final walkthrough

Budget $400–700 for a quality inspector and attend in person. Appraisal protects your lender; if it comes in low you renegotiate or walk — your inspection contingency is your parachute.

## 6. Closing day

Bring ID, wire funds *by verified wire only* (call the title company to confirm instructions — wire fraud targets buyers), and sign roughly your body weight in disclosures. Keys are yours.

> **The 2026 twist:** rate buydowns are back. Ask your lender what 1–2 points would do to your monthly payment versus simply lowering price — sometimes the seller will fund it.

Total timeline from pre-approval to keys: six to ten weeks for most first-timers. Slower than the shows suggest, faster than you fear.
