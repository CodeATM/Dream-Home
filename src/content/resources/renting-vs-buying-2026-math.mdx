The rent-versus-buy debate gets argued with feelings. Let's use arithmetic instead.

## The five-year rule of thumb

Buying usually wins if you'll stay **five-plus years** — enough time for appreciation and principal paydown to overwhelm transaction costs (closing costs in, agent fees out: roughly 8–10% round trip). Under three years, renting almost always wins.

## Run your own break-even

For a typical $600,000 home at current rates with 20% down:

- Monthly P&I: ~$3,190
- Taxes + insurance + maintenance: ~$1,150
- **True monthly cost: ~$4,340**
- Offset by principal paydown (~$640/month) and expected appreciation (~$500/month at 3%)
- **Effective cost: ~$3,200**

If an equivalent rental costs more than ~$3,200, buying wins on pure math. If it's dramatically less, renting is funding your index fund instead of your equity — also respectable.

## What the renter's side of the ledger looks like

Renting isn't burning money; it's buying *optionality*. The down payment invested in a boring S&P 500 fund historically returns ~7% real. Renters also skip maintenance surprises, HOA politics, and being geographically locked to one job market.

## Where each side wins in our cities

**Buy:** Chicago, Nashville, Portland — purchase prices still sit near rental parity.
**Lean rent first:** San Diego, Seattle, Miami — price-to-rent ratios stretch past 25; patience has been paying.

## The verdict

Run both scenarios honestly, including what you'd do with the down payment if you kept renting. Then decide with numbers, not dinner-party pressure. And when you're ready, our [rent affordability calculator](/listings) lives on every rental listing.
