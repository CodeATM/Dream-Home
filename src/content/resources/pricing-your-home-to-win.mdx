The single biggest pricing mistake hasn't changed: listing at the number you want instead of the number the market will defend. In today's market, well-priced homes still go under contract inside three weeks; overpriced ones sit, go stale, and eventually sell for less than they would have at launch.

## Read comps like an appraiser

Pull the last six months of closed sales within a half mile, same bed count, similar vintage. Ignore list prices — closings are truth. Adjust for differences: finished basement adds value, corner lot adds some, busy road subtracts.

## Price bands matter more than precision

Search filters jump in $25K and $50K steps. A home listed at $749,000 shows up in searches up to $750,000; at $755,000 you vanish from a whole band of buyers. Land inside the band, not just near it.

## The pre-listing punch list

Spend where buyers look:

- Paint: the highest-return dollar in real estate.
- Lighting: swap every builder bulb for warm LEDs, fix dead fixtures.
- Landscape edges: mulch and trimmed shrubs photograph like new construction.
- Depersonalize, don't sterilize — buyers want to imagine *their* life, not a showroom.

## Strategy for the first fourteen days

Your first two weekends get the highest traffic you'll ever see. Price to draw the crowd, review feedback Tuesday, and if you're getting showings but no offers by day fourteen, that's data: adjust once, decisively. Three small cuts read as desperation; one confident cut reads as correction.

## Credits beat cuts when appraisals loom

If the appraisal comes in low, a $10,000 closing-cost credit solves the gap without re-papering the loan. Talk to your agent about structuring room for this before you list.

Sellers who follow this playbook in 2026 are clearing close to asking in most of our markets. The ones chasing last year's peak are making their buyer's year.
