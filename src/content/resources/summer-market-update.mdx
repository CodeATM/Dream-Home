Spring brought the thaw. After two years of frozen inventory, new listings rose 11% year over year in our eight metro markets — the first real relief buyers have seen since 2021.

## What changed

Sellers who locked 2.75% mortgages are finally moving — life events beat rate math eventually. Add new-build deliveries ramping across Sun Belt suburbs, and supply finally met demand somewhere near eye level.

## Numbers worth knowing (June, all markets)

- Median days on market: **28** (down from 34 last summer)
- Share of listings with price cuts: **31%** — negotiation is back
- Average sale-to-list ratio: **98.6%**
- Multiple-offer rate: **18%**, concentrated under $500K

## For buyers

You have leverage you haven't had in four years. Use it:

- Ask for closing-cost credits — sellers are saying yes at triple last year's rate.
- Inspection contingencies are standard again; waiving one now reads as desperation, not strength.
- Watch the 31% getting cut — yesterday's overpriced listing is this month's value buy.

## For sellers

The market pays for preparation again. Homes that launched priced to comps cleared within three weeks; homes chasing last June's peak sat and cut twice. If you're selling this year, list early in the month, invest in paint and photos, and price for the first weekend's traffic.

## Outlook

Rates have settled into a band rather than a trend. Our house view: gradual drift down through fall, sticky inventory improvement, and a normal-ish autumn market — which, after the past four years, counts as good news for everyone.
